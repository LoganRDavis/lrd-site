Privacy Policy

Applies to the application lrdlocal-nas-backup. Last updated 25 August 2026.

What this application is

lrdlocal-nas-backup is a private, single-user utility that backs up files from its owner's home server to that same owner's Google Drive account. It is not a product, is not offered to the public, and has no users other than its owner.

Google account data

The application requests the drive.file scope only. This scope restricts it to files and folders it creates itself; it cannot read or modify any other content in the Drive account. It is used solely to upload backup archives and to delete backup archives it previously uploaded.

The application does not read personal Drive content, does not access contacts, mail, calendar, or profile information beyond what is required to complete OAuth authentication, and performs no analytics or profiling.

Data collected from others

None. The application has no web interface, no sign-up, no telemetry, and no third-party users. No data belonging to any person other than the owner is collected, processed, stored, or transmitted.

How data is handled

Backup content is encrypted on the owner's own hardware before upload, so what is stored in Google Drive is ciphertext. OAuth credentials are held only on the owner's server, readable solely by the system administrator account.

Sharing

Nothing is sold, shared, transferred, or disclosed to any third party. The only external service involved is Google Drive, acting as the storage destination.

Retention and deletion

Backup data is retained until superseded or deleted by the owner. Access can be revoked at any time from the owner's Google Account permissions page, which immediately ends the application's access.

This website

This site is static. It uses no analytics, advertising, or tracking cookies, and collects no personal information from visitors.

Contact

Questions may be directed to the address listed as the developer contact on the application's Google OAuth consent screen, or to logan@loganrdavis.com.
